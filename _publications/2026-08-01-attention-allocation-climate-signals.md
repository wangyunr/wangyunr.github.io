### Abstract

Attention allocation to climate information affects whether climate signals become salient enough to enter public discourse on corporate sustainability. ESG provides an institutional framework through which perceived climate change and associated risks can be translated into expectations for corporate environmental responsibility. In an increasingly fragmented climate governance landscape, this study uses city-day mobile search data from China to examine how different climate signals drive ESG-related public interest. Based on 2SLS, the estimates show that physical temperature fluctuations and online climate information increase ESG-related search activity, but their effects differ substantially. A one-unit increase in daily minimum temperature raises the ESG search index by 0.12 units, whereas the effect of online climate information is roughly 10 times larger, suggesting that clearer, more accurate climate signals generate stronger public responses. Regional heterogeneity analysis shows that this mechanism is shaped by local conditions. Eastern provinces exhibit the strongest responses to both online climate information and temperature variation, whereas online information effects are larger inland and temperature effects are stronger along the coast. Educational development further amplifies responses to climate information. Overall, the findings highlight signal characteristics and local conditions that shape coordination demand as key forces driving ESG-related public interest, offering implications for climate communication and sustainability policy design.
